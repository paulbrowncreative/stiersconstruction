Marine and Construction Services Across Metro Detroit’s Waterfront

We serve waterfront communities on Lake St. Clair, the St. Clair River, the Detroit River and Lake Erie, along with the excavating, concrete, welding and deck work on the lots beside them.

Where We Work

Lake St. Clair sits between the St. Clair River to the north and the Detroit River to the south, and the four waterways behave differently. Depth, current, ship traffic and ice change how a wall, dock or piling should be built. Pick your community below for local details, or read how your waterway changes your seawall and dock.

Not sure we cover your address? Call and ask. Our team works for homeowners, commercial property owners and marinas.

Lake St. Clair

Lake St. Clair is shallow, and its northern arm, Anchor Bay, is only about 1 to 11 feet deep across most of its area. Canals, marinas and open lakefront mean walls, docks, hoists and dredging all come up, and record high water in 2019 and 2020 tested shoreline structures.

  • St. Clair ShoresMacomb County. Canal-front lots, boat wells and marinas along Lake St. Clair make St. Clair Shores one of Metro Detroit’s busiest boating communities.
  • Grosse Pointe ShoresWayne County. Open Lake St. Clair frontage in the smallest of the Grosse Pointe communities calls for walls built to handle waves, ice and high water.
  • Grosse Pointe FarmsWayne County. Lakefront homes on the “point” of Grosse Pointe face a shoreline that bends toward the east, so wall design starts with how waves reach your lot.
  • Grosse Pointe ParkWayne County. Where Lake St. Clair narrows into the Detroit River, Grosse Pointe Park pairs lakefront property with older homes and a busy shoreline.
  • Grosse Pointe WoodsWayne County. Grosse Pointe Woods is the one Pointe without its own Lake St. Clair shoreline, so most projects here are concrete, decks, excavating and steel.
  • Harrison TownshipMacomb County. With the Clinton River mouth, Lake St. Clair Metropark and dozens of marinas, Harrison Township is built around the water.
  • New BaltimoreMacomb County. New Baltimore sits on Anchor Bay, a huge but very shallow part of Lake St. Clair where depth is the first question for docks and hoists.
  • Chesterfield TownshipMacomb County. Chesterfield Township borders Anchor Bay, where shallow water, tributary sediment and open-bay waves shape every waterfront project.

St. Clair River

The St. Clair River runs at roughly 1.3 to 2.1 mph between Algonac and Marysville, according to NOAA, and faster near the Blue Water Bridge at Port Huron. Ships pass close to shore and river ice is a winter fact of life, so anchoring, scour resistance and protected steel matter.

  • AlgonacSt. Clair County. Algonac sits where the St. Clair River spreads into the St. Clair Flats delta, so current, ship traffic and island access all come into play.
  • Marine CitySt. Clair County. Freighters pass close to Marine City’s downtown waterfront, and river ice is part of every winter, so walls and docks here work hard.
  • St. ClairSt. Clair County. St. Clair’s riverfront is a long, public boardwalk edge, and private shoreline here deals with steady river flow and passing freighters.
  • MarysvilleSt. Clair County. Marysville’s riverfront includes a living shoreline promenade, and the St. Clair River’s current and ice shape what works on private property.
  • Port HuronSt. Clair County. Port Huron is where Lake Huron drains into the St. Clair River, and the fast water near the Blue Water Bridge is a design factor of its own.

Detroit River

The Detroit River is a busy shipping channel linking Lake St. Clair with Lake Erie. Downriver communities combine river frontage with canals, channels and islands, so current, wakes, silt, ice and island access all shape the work.

  • Grosse IleWayne County. Grosse Ile is the largest island in the Detroit River, and almost every property on it has a shoreline, a canal or a channel to plan around.
  • WyandotteWayne County. Wyandotte’s Detroit River shoreline puts seawalls, docks and pilings up against current, shipping traffic and ice.
  • TrentonWayne County. Trenton faces the Trenton Channel of the Detroit River, where channel current, islands and ice affect every dock and wall.
  • GibraltarWayne County. Canals cut through Gibraltar’s shoreline near the mouth of the Detroit River, so seawalls, boat wells and dredging are everyday concerns.

Lake Erie and Tributaries

At the region’s southern edge, Lake Erie brings open-water waves and level swings, while tributary rivers such as the River Raisin and Huron River add sediment, current and different permit rules.

  • RockwoodWayne County. Rockwood’s waterfront is on the Huron River, which empties into Lake Erie, so river banks, docks and drainage lead the project list.
  • MonroeMonroe County. Monroe is Michigan’s only Lake Erie port city, with the River Raisin running through town and shallow, exposed water at its mouth.

What Waterfront Owners Should Know About Permits

Work in or over Great Lakes waters generally involves state and federal permits. In Michigan that means EGLE, and often the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, and your city or township may add its own rules. EGLE’s shoreline protection permit guidance (PDF) explains the process. Requirements depend on your site, so confirm before work starts.
