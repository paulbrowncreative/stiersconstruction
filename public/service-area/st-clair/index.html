Waterfront Construction in St. Clair

The city of St. Clair fronts the St. Clair River, with Palmer Park and its riverside boardwalk drawing visitors to watch passing ships. NOAA’s Coast Pilot lists current at St. Clair of roughly 1.4 to 2.1 mph depending on river flow, among the higher readings on the lower river.

Steady flow makes scour a real design concern for walls and pilings, and freighter wakes and ice add more load. For homeowners, the practical work includes sound anchoring, protected steel, working drainage and periodic inspection. Concrete steps, patios and decks near the river need to shed water and survive freeze-thaw cycles.

Permits and Local Rules in St. Clair

The St. Clair River is a federally navigated connecting waterway, and work in or over the water generally involves EGLE and the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers. EGLE district staff can tell you which part of Michigan law applies to your site, such as Part 325 (Great Lakes Submerged Lands) or Part 301 (Inland Lakes and Streams), and applications go through the MiEnviro Portal.

Above the waterline, the city building department handles building permits, zoning and setbacks for decks, patios, retaining walls and demolition, and earth changes near water may need a soil erosion and sedimentation control permit through your county or local enforcing agency. Confirm requirements before work starts.

Do Freighter Wakes Affect Docks and Walls on the St. Clair River in St. Clair?

Large ships pass close to the shore, and their wakes and the steady current add load to walls, pilings and docks. Designs account for that with sound anchoring and appropriately protected materials. Tell us how your frontage is exposed when you request a quote.
