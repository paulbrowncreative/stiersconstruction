Waterfront Construction in St. Clair Shores

St. Clair Shores calls itself the Boating Capital of Michigan, and the waterfront backs it up. A network of canals gives many homeowners direct lake access from their backyards, and the Nautical Mile on Jefferson Avenue between Nine and Ten Mile roads is lined with marinas and boat services. Each of those canal lots depends on a seawall, and most also have a dock, boat well or hoist.

Canal walls face a different kind of wear than open shoreline. Constant boat traffic, narrow lots and shallow water stress caps, seams and anchors, and silt builds up in boat wells over the years. Because lots are tight, equipment access often decides the plan: work can run from the land side, from a barge in the canal, or both.

St. Clair Shores at a Glance

  • St. Clair Shores is in Macomb County on Lake St. Clair.
  • The city has both private and city-owned marinas, concentrated along the Nautical Mile.
  • Many residential lots sit on canals, so seawalls, boat wells, docks and hoists are part of everyday home maintenance.

Permits and Local Rules in St. Clair Shores

Work at or below the ordinary high-water mark on Lake St. Clair (574.7 feet on the 1955 datum, or 575.3 feet on the 1985 datum) generally needs a permit from Michigan EGLE and often the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers. Applications go through EGLE’s MiEnviro Portal, which forwards them to the Corps.

Above the waterline, the city building department handles building permits, zoning and setbacks for decks, patios, retaining walls and demolition, and earth changes near water may need a soil erosion and sedimentation control permit through your county or local enforcing agency. Confirm requirements before work starts.
