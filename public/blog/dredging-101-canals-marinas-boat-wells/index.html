Dredging 101: When Canals, Marinas and Boat Wells Need It

Learn the signs a canal, marina or boat well needs dredging, how dredging works, and what permits to expect on Lake St. Clair and the Detroit River.

Canals, marinas and boat wells collect sediment over time. Runoff, wave action and river currents all bring silt that settles where the water is calm. Eventually the water is too shallow for your boat. Dredging is how you get it back.

Key Takeaways

  • Silt build-up reduces depth, makes boats touch bottom and limits navigation.
  • Dredging removes sediment and debris to restore proper depth.
  • Dredging on Great Lakes waters generally needs permits from EGLE, with federal review by the Army Corps.
  • Plan dredging together with seawalls, docks and hoists.

What Is Dredging?

Dredging removes sediment and debris from the bed of a waterway to restore depth and navigation. On residential canals and boat wells, that often means an excavator working from a barge or from shore. Larger marina jobs may use other equipment. Methods depend on the site, the amount of material and access.

Signs You May Need Dredging

  • Your boat touches bottom at the dock or in the slip.
  • The propeller stirs up mud when you leave.
  • Boats that used to clear easily now wait for higher water.
  • Your canal or channel has visibly shoaled.
  • Your boat lift no longer lowers fully clear of the bottom.

Who Dredges?

Homeowners with a boat well or slip, commercial waterfront properties and marinas all dredge. Canal, marina and boat well dredging are the main categories.

Permits

Dredging on Michigan’s Great Lakes bottomlands generally requires an EGLE permit, and federal review by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers may also apply. Sediment handling can be part of the review, and materials may need testing before they are moved or disposed of. Ask EGLE early and plan around the review time. Our permit guide explains the process.

Plan Dredging With Your Other Structures

Dredging near a seawall or dock changes the depth at its base. The wall or pilings have to be designed for that depth. If you plan to dredge and rebuild a wall, coordinate them so the wall is designed for the new bottom. See our seawall anatomy guide, and read how lake levels play into depth problems in Great Lakes water levels.

What to Ask a Dredging Contractor

  • How will you access the site: from land, a barge or both?
  • How will you protect my seawall, pilings and dock?
  • What permits does this need, and who applies?
  • How will the removed material be handled?
  • How will you check final depth?

How a Dredging Project Is Planned

  1. Define the goal. What depth do you need, and where: boat well, slip, channel or the whole canal?
  2. Survey the bottom. Depth measurements show how much material there is and where.
  3. Check permits. Dredging is regulated on Great Lakes waters. See the permit guide.
  4. Choose a method and access. Barge-mounted or shore-based equipment, depending on the site.
  5. Plan sediment handling. Where will the removed material go, and does it need testing?
  6. Protect structures. Seawalls, pilings and docks near the work need consideration.
  7. Verify depth at the end.

Protecting Your Seawall and Pilings

Digging too close to a wall or pile can remove support at its base. Good dredging plans keep safe distances or account for the change in depth. If your wall is old, consider inspecting it first. See seawall anatomy.

What Affects Dredging Costs

  • Volume of material and how deep the cut is
  • Site access and distance to disposal or handling areas
  • Sediment testing or handling requirements
  • Permit requirements and timing
  • Equipment needed, including barge work
  • Protection of nearby structures

Common Dredging Mistakes

  • Dredging without checking permits
  • Cutting too close to a seawall or piling
  • Not surveying first, so the depth goal is a guess
  • Ignoring why sediment is building up, such as runoff or erosion upstream

Reducing Future Silt

You may not stop siltation completely, but keeping your seawall and drainage in good condition, controlling erosion on your lot and maintaining shoreline structures can help. See the seasonal checklist.

Frequently Asked Questions

How Deep Should My Boat Well Be?

Deep enough for your boat’s draft plus a margin, and for your lift to lower fully. Your boat’s specifications and your contractor can help set a target depth.

How Often Does a Canal Need Dredging?

It varies with sediment supply, currents and storms. Shallow water and grounding are the practical signals.
