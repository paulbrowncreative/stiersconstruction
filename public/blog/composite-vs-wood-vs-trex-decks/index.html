Composite vs. Wood vs. Trex Decks for Lakefront and Canal-Front Homes

Compare composite, wood and Trex decking for waterfront homes, plus pool deck safety, footings and maintenance in Michigan’s climate.

A deck is where waterfront living happens. It is also constantly exposed to moisture, sun and Michigan’s freeze-thaw cycles. Here is how the main decking options compare.

Key Takeaways

  • Composite resists rot, warping and insects with minimal upkeep. Wood offers classic beauty and customization but needs periodic sealing or staining.
  • Trex is a premium composite brand known for durability, fade resistance and low maintenance.
  • Pool decks should use slip-resistant, moisture-resistant surfaces.
  • Footings in Michigan typically go below the frost line. Your local building official decides.

Composite Decking

Composite decking is made from a blend of wood fibers and recycled plastic. It offers the look of wood with minimal maintenance and resists rot, warping and insect damage. It suits homeowners who want a long-lasting deck without frequent upkeep.

Wood Decking

Real lumber offers a classic, natural look and a warm, traditional feel. It is highly customizable and can be shaped, stained or painted. The trade-off is periodic maintenance like sealing or staining, so it suits homeowners who love the natural aesthetic and are willing to do it.

Trex

Trex is a premium brand of composite decking known for its durability, fade resistance and eco-friendliness. It suits families and busy homeowners who want a high-end, low-maintenance deck with style and longevity. (Trex® is a registered trademark of Trex Company, Inc.)

Pool Decks

Pool decks can be wood, composite or Trex, but safety leads: slip-resistant, moisture-resistant surfaces suit wet environments. The right material depends on your aesthetic preference, maintenance tolerance and how often the deck will be exposed to water.

Footings and Frost

Frost can lift footings, and Michigan building departments typically require footings to extend at least 42 inches below grade, though your local building official decides and may require permits and inspections. Decks attached to a house have to follow local code. Ask your contractor to walk you through it.

Railings and Stairs

Railings and stairs add safety and style. Steel railings can be fabricated to match your deck, house and setting. See welding and fabrication.

Decks Along Seawalls

Decks near a wall or dock need to allow for drainage and access, and structures close to the water may trigger extra permit requirements. Read our permit guide.

Framing, Fasteners and Moisture

The surface is only part of a deck. Framing and fasteners live in a damp environment near water, so material and hardware choices matter. Ask your contractor about the framing material, corrosion-resistant fasteners and flashing where the deck meets the house. Good ventilation and drainage under the deck also help.

Long-Term Maintenance

  • Wood: plan to seal or stain periodically, and inspect for splits and loose fasteners.
  • Composite and Trex: clean regularly and check fasteners. Follow manufacturer guidance.
  • All decks: clear debris between boards and keep drainage paths open.

Design Ideas for Waterfront Decks

  • Multi-level decks that step down toward the water
  • Built-in seating that takes advantage of views
  • Wide stairs for easy access to a patio or dock
  • Steel or cable railings that keep views open
  • Non-slip surfaces near pool or boat areas

Permits and Inspections

Decks generally need permits and inspections, particularly when attached to a house or built at height. Your local building department sets requirements. Structures close to the water may involve other rules. Ask your contractor who applies and what inspections apply.

Repairs and Upgrades

Existing decks can often be repaired or upgraded: new decking, railings, stairs or framing repairs. A contractor can tell you whether the structure is sound enough to build on.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Composite Worth It Over Wood?

If you want minimal upkeep and long life, composite is attractive. If you love natural lumber and do not mind sealing or staining, wood is a good fit.

Do I Need a Permit for a Deck?

Usually your city or township requires a building permit for decks. Check with your local building department.
