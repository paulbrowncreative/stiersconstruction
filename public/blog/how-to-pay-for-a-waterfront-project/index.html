How to Pay for a Seawall, Dock or Waterfront Project

Options for financing a seawall, dock, hoist or deck project, and what to compare before you borrow: APR, fees, term and approval terms.

A new seawall, dock or deck is a significant investment, and repairs sometimes come at inconvenient times. If you would rather not pay everything at once, there are several ways to spread out the cost. This article is general information, not financial advice.

Key Takeaways

  • Waterfront projects can be large. Financing can spread the cost into monthly payments.
  • Compare the total cost of any loan: APR, fees, term and payment.
  • Financing offers are subject to credit approval, and terms vary by lender.
  • Get a written quote first. Every financing request starts from the project price.

Start With a Quote

Any financing decision begins with a clear price. Ask for a written quote that lists the work, materials and permits so you know how much you need. Request a free quote.

Common Ways to Pay

  • Savings or cash. No interest, but it may drain reserves.
  • Home equity products, such as a home equity line of credit, which use your home as collateral.
  • Personal loans, which are unsecured and based on your creditworthiness.
  • Financing arranged through your contractor, often through a marketplace that matches you with lenders.

Offers are subject to credit approval, and rates and terms vary by lender. You are not required to accept any offer. See our financing page for how it works.

Before You Borrow

  1. Read every offer’s terms carefully.
  2. Compare more than one option.
  3. Consider talking to a financial professional about your situation.
  4. Only borrow an amount that fits your budget.

Questions to Ask Any Lender

  • What is the APR, and is it fixed or variable?
  • What fees are there, and when are they charged?
  • What is the term, and what is the total repayment amount?
  • Is there a penalty for paying early?
  • Is the loan secured by my home or another asset?
  • Will you run a hard credit check, and when?
  • What happens if I miss a payment?

Phasing a Project

Sometimes a large project can be split into stages: for example, a seawall first, then a dock or deck. Ask your contractor whether phasing makes sense technically, and how it would affect cost and permits.

Repair Now or Replace Later?

Maintenance and repairs can extend a wall’s or dock’s life, but not always. If repairs cost a large fraction of replacement, or the underlying problem will not be solved, replacement may be a better use of money. A written assessment helps you decide. See warning signs of seawall failure.

Keep Records

Keep your quote, permits, contract, receipts and warranty information together. They matter when you sell or file an insurance claim.
