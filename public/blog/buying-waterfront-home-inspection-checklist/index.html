Buying a Waterfront Home in Metro Detroit: A Seawall, Dock and Hoist Inspection Checklist

Before you buy waterfront property, check the seawall, dock, pilings, hoist and drainage. Use this inspection checklist and know what to ask.

Waterfront homes come with structures that inland homes do not: seawalls, docks, pilings and boat lifts. They are easy to overlook during a showing, and expensive to fix after closing. This checklist helps you look at them with a buyer’s eye. It is general guidance, not legal or inspection advice.

Key Takeaways

  • A standard home inspection does not usually cover seawalls, docks, pilings or hoists. Consider a specialist.
  • Ask for permit records, repair history and photos of the shoreline structures.
  • Seawall replacement can be a major expense. Learn the condition before you close.
  • Take photos and notes so you can negotiate with facts.

Before You Make an Offer

  • Ask for records. Request permits, repair invoices and any warranty paperwork for the seawall, dock, hoist and dredging.
  • Ask about problems. Has the wall been repaired? Has the property seen flooding or erosion?
  • Look at the water level. Levels vary. See Great Lakes water levels.
  • Involve your agent and attorney in questions about permits, property lines and waterfront rights.

Seawall Checklist

  • Does the wall lean or bow? Sight down the top from the end.
  • Are there sinkholes, depressions or voids behind the wall?
  • Are seams open, or is soil or staining visible at joints?
  • Is the cap cracked, loose or missing?
  • Is there erosion at the wall ends or where it meets neighbors?
  • Does water pool near the wall? Is there any drainage?
  • What is it made of, and how old is it?

Details in 7 warning signs your seawall is failing and the seawall guide.

Dock and Piling Checklist

  • Is the decking solid, with no soft or rotting boards?
  • Are the pilings straight, secure and free of heavy corrosion or rot?
  • Do the dock and gangway move more than they should when you walk on them?
  • Are piling covers in place?

See wood vs. steel pilings and floating vs. fixed docks.

Boat Hoist Checklist

  • What type and size of lift is it, and what boat does it fit? See how to choose a boat hoist.
  • Are the cables frayed or corroded?
  • Does the motor run smoothly?
  • Are frame welds and supports free of cracks and heavy rust?

Water Depth and Access

  • Can your boat get in and out? Shallow slips may need dredging.
  • Is the boat well or slip clear of debris?

Permits and Compliance

  • Do permits exist for the existing structures? Work on Great Lakes waters generally needs EGLE and often Army Corps approval. See the permit guide.
  • Are there open permit issues?

Questions to Ask the Seller

  • When was the seawall built or last repaired, and by whom?
  • Has the wall, dock or hoist had problems, and how were they resolved?
  • Are permits available for existing structures?
  • Has the property flooded or seen erosion?
  • When was the slip or boat well last dredged?
  • Which structures, such as docks and lifts, convey with the sale?

Red Flags

Red FlagWhy It Matters
Leaning or bowing wallMay indicate anchor or footing problems
Fresh fill or patching behind the wallCould hide a void or recurring settling
No permit records for structuresCould mean unpermitted work
Spongy dock decking, tilted pilingsSuggests deterioration
Frayed lift cables, heavy rust on framesSafety and repair cost
Shallow slip or boat wellMay need dredging before you can use your boat

Planning for Cost

Seawall replacement, dock rebuilding and dredging can be large projects. Getting a contractor’s opinion before closing helps you budget or negotiate. Ask for a written scope and quote. If financing the work afterward, see our financing page and how to pay for a waterfront project.

Timing Your Inspections

Try to see the property when water is calm and levels are typical. Winter snow and ice can hide problems, so ask for photos from other seasons. A marine contractor’s inspection can be scheduled during your inspection period.

Get a Specialist Opinion

If the wall, dock or hoist shows problems, or if you want peace of mind, ask a marine contractor to look at it. Photos and notes from your visit help. Stier’s Construction can review photos of seawalls, docks and hoists and quote repair or replacement. See seawalls or request a free quote.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a Home Inspector Check the Seawall?

Standard inspections often focus on the house. Ask specifically whether the seawall, dock and hoist are covered, and consider a marine contractor for those.

Can I Negotiate Repairs Into the Purchase?

Often, yes, when you have documentation. Talk to your agent and attorney about how to handle it.
