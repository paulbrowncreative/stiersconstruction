7 Warning Signs Your Seawall Is Failing (and What to Do Next)

Leaning walls, sinkholes, open seams and cap cracks are early signs of seawall trouble. Learn what to look for and when to call a contractor.

Most seawall failures give plenty of warning. The trick is knowing what to look for. Walk your shoreline in spring and fall with a phone, and check for these seven signs.

Key Takeaways

  • Early signs include leaning, sinking soil, open seams, cap damage, end erosion, pooling water and gaps between wall and soil.
  • Catching problems early can turn a replacement into a repair.
  • Take dated photos. They help a contractor judge how fast a problem is changing.

1. the Wall Leans or Bows

Stand at one end of the wall and sight along its top. A straight wall that now leans toward the water, or bulges in the middle, is being pushed by soil or water pressure or has lost its anchoring. This is the most serious sign.

2. Soil Is Sinking Behind the Wall

Depressions, sinkholes or settling near the wall often mean soil is escaping through gaps or openings in the wall. If you can push a rod into voids behind the cap, note where and take photos.

3. Seams Are Open or Separating

Sheet piling interlocks. When seams open, soil and water pass through, and the wall can lose material a little at a time. Look for staining or sediment on the water side at seam lines.

4. the Cap Is Cracked, Loose or Missing

The cap ties the top of the wall together and sheds water. Cracks, loose sections or missing pieces let water in and weaken the wall. Cap repairs or replacement can be a relatively small fix when caught early.

5. Erosion at the Ends of the Wall

Water can find its way around the end of a wall and eat into the soil behind it (this is called flanking). Look for washouts at the wall’s ends and where it meets a neighbor’s wall.

6. Water Pools Near the Wall After Rain

Standing water near the wall, or wet ground that never dries, suggests poor drainage. Water pressure builds behind walls that cannot drain. French drains relieve that pressure, and installing or clearing them is a common repair.

7. Gaps Between the Wall and the Soil

A visible gap or crack along the top of the wall means the soil has moved away from it, or the wall has moved away from the soil. Either way, something is shifting.

What to Do If You See One

  1. Take dated photos and repeat them monthly. Change over time tells a contractor a lot.
  2. Keep heavy loads back. Avoid parking vehicles or stacking materials near the wall.
  3. Do not backfill and hope. Filling a void hides the symptom without fixing the cause.
  4. Call a marine contractor for an inspection, especially if the wall is leaning or soil is sinking.
  5. Ask about permits early. Replacement generally needs EGLE and often Army Corps approval. See the permit guide.

A Step-by-Step Seawall Self-Inspection

  1. Choose dry, daylight conditions. Low water makes it easier to see the wall face.
  2. Start at one end and sight down the top of the wall. Look for waves in the line.
  3. Walk the length and look at the cap for cracks, gaps and missing sections.
  4. Look at the ground within a few feet of the wall for dips, holes and cracks.
  5. Look at the wall face from a boat or dock if you can, for staining, sediment and open seams.
  6. Check the ends and where your wall meets a neighbor’s.
  7. Look at drainage: where does water go after rain?
  8. Take photos of anything unusual, with something for scale.

Do not lean over the edge, stand on questionable ground or enter the water. If it looks unstable, keep back and call a professional.

Why Waiting Costs More

Once soil starts escaping, the loss accelerates. Voids grow, the wall loses support and repairs become larger. Early action can turn a wall replacement into a repair, or at least keep a small replacement from becoming a large one.

Photos That Help a Contractor

  • A wide shot of the full wall from each end
  • Close-ups of cracks, gaps and seams, with a coin or tape for scale
  • The ground behind the wall showing sinkholes or settling
  • Any docks, hoists or structures connected to the wall
  • The same views again a month later if something is changing

Repair or Replace?

Seam repair, cap work, splash guards and drainage can extend the life of a sound wall. A wall that has lost its footing or shifted significantly usually has to be replaced. Stier’s Construction handles both. See seawall repair and installation, or send us photos for a free quote.

Frequently Asked Questions

How Often Should I Inspect My Seawall?

Walk it at least twice a year, in spring after the thaw and in fall before freeze-up, and after any major storm or high-water event.

Is a Small Crack in the Cap a Big Deal?

Not always, but cracks let water in. Freeze-thaw can widen them, so it is worth having a contractor look, particularly if you see other signs.
