How Michigan Winters Damage Docks and Seawalls (and How to Prepare)

Ice, freeze-thaw and frozen ground stress docks, hoists and seawalls. Learn how winter damage happens and how to prepare your waterfront.

Michigan waterfronts spend part of the year frozen. Ice, shifting water levels and freeze-thaw cycles put stresses on docks, hoists and seawalls that summer never does. Here is how winter damage happens, and what you can do about it.

Key Takeaways

  • Ice pushes on walls, grips pilings and can lift or shift docks and hoists.
  • Freeze-thaw cycles widen cracks in caps, concrete and seams.
  • Drainage matters in winter too: trapped water freezes and expands.
  • Fall inspections and prep are cheaper than spring emergency repairs.

How Winter Damages Waterfront Structures

Ice Pressure

When ice forms and expands or moves with wind and current, it pushes on seawalls, pilings and docks. That can shift or bow walls and damage dock frames.

Ice Grip on Pilings

Ice can freeze to a piling. If the water level rises or falls, the ice can pull the piling up or twist it. Pilings that seem to have moved in spring may have been lifted by ice.

Freeze-Thaw Cycles

Water gets into small cracks in caps, concrete and seams, then freezes and expands. Repeated cycles widen the cracks. This is why small cracks are worth sealing or patching before winter. See waterfront concrete and freeze-thaw.

Trapped Water Behind Walls

Water that cannot drain from behind a wall can freeze and add to the pressure on it. French drains help. See seawall anatomy.

Frozen Ground

Frost can heave footings and posts. Municipal building departments in Michigan typically require footings to extend at least 42 inches below grade, though your local building official decides. This matters for decks and structures near the water.

Fall Preparation Checklist

  1. Walk the seawall. Note cracks, gaps, sinkholes and cap damage. Repair what you can before the freeze. See seawall warning signs.
  2. Clear drainage. Make sure French drains and surface drainage are working.
  3. Plan your dock. Decide whether sections come out for the winter, and follow the manufacturer’s guidance.
  4. Prepare your hoist. Follow your manufacturer’s winter instructions. See hoist maintenance.
  5. Protect pilings. Check piling covers and note anything that needs repair. See wood vs. steel pilings.
  6. Take photos so you can compare in spring.

Spring Check-up

After the thaw, walk the shoreline again. Compare against your fall photos. Look for new leaning, gaps, heaved pilings and cracked caps. Schedule repairs early, since spring is a busy season for waterfront contractors.

Winter Work Is Possible

Marine work does not stop entirely in winter. Our project photos include jobs done in snow and on ice. Ask about scheduling for your project.

Managing Ice Around Docks

Owners use several approaches to reduce ice damage. Some remove or lower dock sections in fall. Others use equipment that keeps water moving around pilings. Whether that suits your site depends on the dock type, water depth, power and local conditions, and some methods may raise permit or safety questions. Talk with your contractor about options that fit your dock.

Snow and Salt

  • Snow load: Heavy snow adds weight to docks, decks and boat house roofs.
  • De-icing salts: Salt can harm concrete and corrode metal. Follow your contractor’s guidance on products and sealers. See waterfront concrete.
  • Snow banks near walls: Plowing snow against a seawall cap adds weight and traps water. Keep piles back where you can.

Winter Safety

Ice on lakes and canals may not be safe to walk on. Do not go onto ice to inspect structures. Wait for spring or hire professionals equipped for winter work.

What to Do If Ice Damages Something

  1. Stay safe, and do not go on the ice.
  2. Take photos from shore.
  3. Check with your insurance provider about coverage if there is significant damage.
  4. Call a marine contractor for an inspection when it is safe.
  5. Ask about permits before making repairs to structures on the water. See the permit guide.

Why Winter Is a Good Time to Plan

Spring is busy. Planning repairs, replacements and permits over winter puts you first in line when the ground thaws. Ask about winter and off-season scheduling.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I Remove My Dock for Winter?

It depends on the dock type and exposure. Follow the manufacturer’s guidance and ask your contractor. Some docks are designed to stay; others are removed or partly removed.

Can Winter Damage Show up Months Later?

Yes. Small cracks and shifts often show up in spring as the ground thaws. Compare against fall photos.
